Donor Visit

Seeing Impact Firsthand at Sheba

Seeing impact firsthand changes the way we think about giving.

During a recent donor visit to Sheba Medical Center, we had the opportunity to see how philanthropy moves from an idea and a commitment into something tangible — advanced medical care, new technologies, better facilities and new possibilities for patients and their families.

What impressed us most was not only what has already been achieved, but what is still being built.

Healthcare is changing rapidly. Technology, research, data and medical innovation are opening doors that only a few years ago seemed impossible. But innovation alone is not enough. It needs people who believe in it, support it and help bring it from an idea into the hands of doctors and patients.

This is very much at the heart of what we are building at DKF. Our ambition is to create a bridge between philanthropy and innovation — supporting healthcare where the need is immediate, while also investing in the technologies, infrastructure and ideas that can shape the medicine of tomorrow.

We want our giving to do more than solve today's challenges. We want it to help build better healthcare for the future.

Because the greatest impact happens when capital, knowledge, technology and purpose come together around one goal: better healthcare, better outcomes, more lives touched.

Community

Giving Back to Those Who Give

Some people give so much of themselves — their time, energy, care and commitment — often quietly, without expecting anything in return. This special evening was created simply to give something back.

For one evening, the focus was on appreciation, generosity and the importance of creating moments that allow people to pause, enjoy and feel seen.

The event took place at a beautiful hotel in Zichron Yaakov, where guests were invited to enjoy the pool and hotel facilities before gathering for a festive cocktail reception. The evening continued with speeches, a rich and varied dinner, a stand-up performance, drinks, thoughtful treats and gifts — all designed to create a warm, joyful and memorable experience.

At DK Foundation, we believe that philanthropy is not only about responding to major needs or supporting long-term initiatives. Sometimes, meaningful giving is also about creating moments of care, recognition and human connection.

Because impact can be measured in many ways — and sometimes a simple gesture of appreciation can stay with someone long after the evening is over.

Health-Tech

Tenasors Medical: Safer, Smarter Endoscopy

Tenasors Medical is developing a new generation of smart medical devices for gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopy — designed to make procedures safer, diagnosis more accurate and treatment more precise.

Its lead product, the WTI (Wall Thickness Indicator), is an advanced probe that combines optical fiber technology, AI-driven analytics and a real-time automated safety stop. It measures the thickness of tissue layers at the micron level within milliseconds, giving physicians precise feedback before they take a biopsy or perform a treatment.

This matters most for patients with inflammatory bowel diseases such as Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. Today, diagnosis relies on physical biopsies and a 5-to-7-day wait for pathology results. These patients' intestinal walls are often inflamed, thin and fragile, which puts them at higher risk of injury during procedures.

Tenasors aims to change that: an “optical biopsy” that profiles intestinal layers and inflammation severity on the spot, the ability to deliver medication directly to the affected site during the same procedure (“see and treat”), and added protection against tissue injury. The company estimates its technology can shorten procedures by up to 30% and increase daily procedural capacity by 20–30%.

Looking ahead, Tenasors is building a platform that can expand to other organs, including the small intestine, stomach and liver, and grow from a diagnostic tool into a full therapeutic and AI-powered decision-support system.

DKF has backed Tenasors from its inception, with DKF founder Alin Kipnis as a cornerstone investor and partner. It is a clear example of the Health-Tech investments we believe in: technology that puts patient safety first and turns medical innovation into better care.

Investments April 2026

Why Healthcare Investment Is a Community Decision

When we invest in medical ventures, we're not chasing returns — we're building the infrastructure of care.

Every Health-Tech fund we back, every clinical space we help create, is a statement: that capital can serve people before profit. At DKF, we believe the strongest investment thesis is one rooted in community — because healthier communities build stronger economies, and stronger economies fund better healthcare. It's a cycle we're proud to fuel.

The traditional model of healthcare investment treats patients as consumers and clinics as revenue centers. We reject that premise. Our approach starts with a question: what does the community need? From there, we work backward — identifying the ventures, technologies, and infrastructure that will deliver the greatest collective benefit.

This isn't philanthropy dressed as investment. It's investment informed by philanthropy. We hold our portfolio companies to rigorous financial standards — but we also hold them to community standards. Access, equity, and measurable patient outcomes are non-negotiable metrics alongside growth and margins.

When capital flows toward community health, everyone benefits. That's not idealism — it's our investment thesis.

Every fund we partner with as LP must demonstrate not only strong financial fundamentals, but a real commitment to improving healthcare outcomes. Our returns don't just sustain DKF — they validate a model where doing good and doing well are the same thing.
